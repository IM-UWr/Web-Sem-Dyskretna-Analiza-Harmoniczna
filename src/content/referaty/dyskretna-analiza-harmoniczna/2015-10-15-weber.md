Just like the theory of C*-algebras can be seen as a non-commutative version
of topology, compact quantum groups arise as a generalization of compact
groups in this framework. Hence, they give rise to a good notion of
symmetries in this context. In 1988, Woronowicz proved a Tannaka-Krein
result for compact (matrix) quantum groups. We first recall and promote this
beautiful theorem and then point out how it opens the door to combinatorial
methods in the theory of quantum groups.

We apply this Tannaka-Krein machinery to obtain Banica and Speicher's
orthogonal easy quantum groups (their work from 2009), and we extend it to
unitary easy quantum groups (joint work in progress with Pierre Tarrago) and
other deformations of the "easy" concept (joint work in progress with
Guillaume Cébron).
